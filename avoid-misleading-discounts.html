Spotting misleading discount labels

Signals that a percentage is doing more work than the actual reduction.

An always-on “was” price

When the crossed-out price is never charged, it is a reference point, not a price. Compare against the normal selling price.

Countdown pressure

Timers reduce the chance you compare, read the terms, or reconsider. A genuine saving is usually still a saving in an hour.

Percentages on inflated figures

The bigger the base, the bigger the percentage looks. Cash differences are honest; percentages can flatter.

Conditions in the small print

“Up to”, “when you spend” and “members only” all limit who qualifies. The headline is the best case.

A calm check

  • What is the normal price?
  • What is the final total?
  • Do I qualify for the advertised reduction?
  • Would I buy it without the label?
